# Sample research chat (synthetic test fixture)

**Me**: what does recent research say about urban heat islands? Cite sources, please.

**Assistant**: Urban heat islands are ==metropolitan areas that run measurably warmer than their rural surroundings because built surfaces absorb and re-emit solar energy==. [1, 2]
The effect is strongest at night and during heat waves, when the temperature gap can widen enough to raise heat-related hospital admissions. [2, 3]
---

## Drivers

Researchers have identified several reliable drivers of the effect:

1. Surface Materials: Dark asphalt and roofing absorb far more shortwave radiation than vegetation. [2]
2. Street Geometry: Narrow street canyons trap long-wave radiation and slow nighttime cooling. [2, 4]
3. Missing Vegetation: Studies of tree canopy show evapotranspiration cools neighborhoods by several degrees. [5]

2. *"The fundamental mismatch is climatic: reflective strategies assume clear skies, while humid cities spend much of the summer under cloud cover..."*

   * *Citation context:* Use this to argue that a single material fix cannot carry a citywide plan.

3. The Equity Gap: In *The Hidden Costs of Cool Roof Mandates* (parallel to studies like *SLOW: Staged Retrofits for Older Housing Stock*), empirical research demonstrates that retrofit costs fall unevenly on renters.
